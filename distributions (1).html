01 Bernoulli Distribution

Single trial with two outcomes — Success (1) or Failure (0)

PMF Formula
P(X = x) = p^x · (1-p)^(1-x), x ∈ {0, 1}
p = probability of success
Mean = p  |  Variance = p(1-p)
Example 1 Credit Card Default Prediction 🏦 Banking

A bank models whether a customer will default on a loan (1) or not (0). Historical data shows 8% default rate.

Question: What is the probability that a randomly selected customer defaults? What is the variance?

Manual Calculation
1.Given: p = 0.08 (8% default rate)
2.P(X=1) = p¹·(1-p)⁰ = 0.08 × 1 = 0.08 (8%)
3.P(X=0) = p⁰·(1-p)¹ = 1 × 0.92 = 0.92 (92%)
4.Mean = p = 0.08
5.Variance = p(1-p) = 0.08 × 0.92 = 0.0736
→ 8% chance of default; Variance = 0.0736 (spread around average)
Example 2 Email Click-Through Rate 📧 E-Commerce

An e-commerce company sends a promotional email. Past data shows 15% of recipients click the offer link.

Question: For one random recipient, model the click (1) or no-click (0) event.

Manual Calculation
1.p = 0.15 (click), 1-p = 0.85 (no click)
2.P(click) = 0.15  |  P(no click) = 0.85
3.E[X] = 0.15 — expected clicks per email sent
4.Var(X) = 0.15 × 0.85 = 0.1275
5.Std Dev = √0.1275 = 0.357 — uncertainty per send
→ On average 15 out of 100 emails get clicked; SD = 0.357
02 Binomial Distribution

Number of successes in n independent Bernoulli trials

PMF Formula
P(X = k) = C(n,k) · p^k · (1-p)^(n-k)
n = number of trials  |  k = number of successes  |  p = success probability
C(n,k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!)  |  Mean = np  |  Variance = np(1-p)
Example 1 Quality Control — Defective Items 🏭 Manufacturing

A factory produces bolts with a 5% defect rate. A batch of 10 bolts is randomly inspected.

Question: What is the probability of finding exactly 2 defective bolts?

Manual Calculation
1.Given: n=10, k=2, p=0.05, (1-p)=0.95
2.C(10,2) = 10! / (2! × 8!) = (10×9)/(2×1) = 45
3.p² = (0.05)² = 0.0025
4.(1-p)⁸ = (0.95)⁸ = 0.6634
5.P(X=2) = 45 × 0.0025 × 0.6634 = 45 × 0.001659 = 0.0746
6.Mean = np = 10 × 0.05 = 0.5 defects expected
→ ~7.5% probability of exactly 2 defective bolts in a batch of 10
Example 2 Sales Conversion Rate 💼 Sales / CRM

A sales rep has a 30% conversion rate. She makes 8 calls today.

Question: What is the probability she converts exactly 3 customers?

Manual Calculation
1.n=8, k=3, p=0.30, (1-p)=0.70
2.C(8,3) = 8!/(3!×5!) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 56
3.p³ = (0.30)³ = 0.027
4.(0.70)⁵ = 0.70×0.70×0.70×0.70×0.70 = 0.16807
5.P(X=3) = 56 × 0.027 × 0.16807 = 56 × 0.004538 = 0.2541
6.Mean = 8×0.30 = 2.4 sales expected; Var = 8×0.3×0.7 = 1.68
→ ~25.4% chance of exactly 3 conversions out of 8 calls
03 Poisson Distribution

Number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space

PMF Formula
P(X = k) = (λ^k · e^(-λ)) / k!
λ (lambda) = average rate of occurrence  |  e ≈ 2.71828  |  k = 0,1,2,...
Mean = λ  |  Variance = λ
Example 1 Server Request Rate ☁️ IT / Cloud

A web server receives an average of 5 requests per minute.

Question: What is the probability of receiving exactly 3 requests in a given minute?

Manual Calculation
1.λ = 5, k = 3
2.λ^k = 5³ = 125
3.e^(-λ) = e^(-5) = 0.006738
4.k! = 3! = 3×2×1 = 6
5.P(X=3) = (125 × 0.006738) / 6 = 0.8422 / 6 = 0.1404
→ ~14% probability of exactly 3 server requests in one minute
Example 2 Hospital Emergency Arrivals 🏥 Healthcare

An ER receives an average of 2 critical patients per hour.

Question: Probability of exactly 4 critical patients arriving in one hour?

Manual Calculation
1.λ = 2, k = 4
2.λ^k = 2⁴ = 16
3.e^(-2) = 0.1353
4.k! = 4! = 4×3×2×1 = 24
5.P(X=4) = (16 × 0.1353) / 24 = 2.165 / 24 = 0.0902
→ ~9% probability of 4 critical arrivals in any given hour — useful for staffing!
04 Normal Distribution

The bell curve — continuous, symmetric distribution around the mean

PDF Formula
f(x) = (1 / σ√(2π)) · exp(-(x-μ)² / 2σ²)
μ = mean  |  σ = standard deviation  |  σ² = variance
68-95-99.7 Rule: P(μ±1σ)=68%, P(μ±2σ)=95%, P(μ±3σ)=99.7%
Example 1 Product Weight Control 🏭 FMCG / Manufacturing

A biscuit company fills packets with mean weight μ = 200g, standard deviation σ = 5g.

Question: What % of packets weigh between 190g and 210g?

Manual Calculation using Z-score
1.For x = 190: Z = (190-200)/5 = -2.0
2.For x = 210: Z = (210-200)/5 = +2.0
3.P(Z < 2.0) from Z-table = 0.9772
4.P(Z < -2.0) from Z-table = 0.0228
5.P(190 < X < 210) = 0.9772 - 0.0228 = 0.9544 = 95.44%
→ ~95.44% of packets fall within ±2σ; only ~4.56% are out of spec
Example 2 Employee Performance Scores 👥 HR / Talent Management

Annual performance scores follow Normal distribution with μ = 70, σ = 10.

Question: What % of employees score above 85 (top performers)?

Manual Calculation
1.Z = (85 - 70) / 10 = 15/10 = 1.5
2.P(Z < 1.5) from Z-table = 0.9332
3.P(X > 85) = 1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668 = 6.68%
→ About 6.68% of employees are top performers (score > 85)
05 Standard Normal Distribution

Special case of Normal: μ = 0, σ = 1. Z-scores transform any normal variable.

Z-score Transformation
Z = (X - μ) / σ     φ(z) = (1/√2π) · e^(-z²/2)
Z ~ N(0,1)  |  Use Z-table to find P(Z < z)  |  Mean = 0  |  Variance = 1
Example 1 Investment Return Risk Assessment 📈 Finance / Risk

A mutual fund has annual return μ = 12%, σ = 6%. Investor wants to know risk of loss (return < 0%).

Manual Calculation
1.Z = (0 - 12) / 6 = -12/6 = -2.0
2.P(Z < -2.0) from Z-table = 0.0228
3.P(loss) = P(X < 0) = 2.28%
→ Only 2.28% chance of losing money — fund is relatively safe

06 Log-Normal Distribution

If ln(X) is normally distributed, then X is log-normally distributed. Always positive, right-skewed.

Key Relationships
If Y = ln(X) ~ N(μ, σ²), then X ~ LogNormal(μ, σ²)
Mean = e^(μ + σ²/2)  |  Median = e^μ  |  Variance = (e^σ² - 1)·e^(2μ+σ²)
To find P(X ≤ x): convert to Z = (ln(x) - μ) / σ, then use Z-table
Example 1 Income Distribution Modeling 💰 Economics / Insurance

Household incomes follow a log-normal distribution with μ = 10.8, σ = 0.9 (in log scale).

Question: What is the median and mean income? (in ₹/year)

Manual Calculation
1.Median = e^μ = e^10.8 = ₹49,021
2.σ²/2 = (0.9)²/2 = 0.81/2 = 0.405
3.Mean = e^(10.8 + 0.405) = e^11.205 = ₹73,583
4.Mean > Median confirms right-skew — high earners pull mean up
→ Median income ₹49K but mean ₹73K — classic income inequality pattern
Example 2 Stock Price Modeling (Black-Scholes) 📊 Quantitative Finance

Daily log returns of a stock: μ = 0.001, σ = 0.02. Current price = ₹500.

Question: What is the expected price after 1 day?

Manual Calculation
1.ln(S_t) = ln(500) + return ~ N(0.001, 0.02²)
2.E[S_t] = S₀ · e^(μ + σ²/2) = 500 · e^(0.001 + 0.0002)
3.= 500 · e^0.0012 = 500 × 1.00120 = ₹500.60
4.P(price > ₹510): Z = (ln(510)-ln(500)-0.001)/0.02 = (0.0198-0.001)/0.02 = 0.94
5.P(Z > 0.94) = 1 - 0.8264 = 17.36%
→ Expected price ₹500.60; only 17.4% chance of exceeding ₹510 next day
07 Exponential Distribution

Time between events in a Poisson process. Memoryless property.

PDF & CDF Formula
f(x) = λ · e^(-λx), x ≥ 0
P(X ≤ x) = 1 - e^(-λx)
λ = rate parameter (events per unit time)  |  Mean = 1/λ  |  Variance = 1/λ²
Memoryless: P(X > s+t | X > s) = P(X > t)
Example 1 Machine Failure / Maintenance Scheduling ⚙️ Industrial Engineering

A CNC machine fails on average once every 200 hours (λ = 1/200 = 0.005 per hour).

Question: Probability the machine runs for more than 300 hours without failure?

Manual Calculation
1.λ = 1/200 = 0.005 per hour  |  x = 300 hours
2.P(X > 300) = e^(-λx) = e^(-0.005 × 300)
3.= e^(-1.5) = 0.2231
4.Mean time between failures = 1/λ = 200 hours
→ Only 22.3% chance machine survives 300 hours without failure — schedule maintenance earlier!
Example 2 Customer Service Wait Time 📞 Telecom / Call Center

A call center handles calls at rate λ = 3 calls per minute. Customers expect short waits.

Question: Probability a customer waits less than 30 seconds (0.5 min) for service?

Manual Calculation
1.λ = 3/min  |  x = 0.5 min
2.P(X ≤ 0.5) = 1 - e^(-3 × 0.5) = 1 - e^(-1.5)
3.e^(-1.5) = 0.2231
4.P(X ≤ 0.5) = 1 - 0.2231 = 0.7769 = 77.69%
5.Mean wait = 1/3 min = 20 seconds
→ ~77.7% of customers are served within 30 seconds — good SLA performance
